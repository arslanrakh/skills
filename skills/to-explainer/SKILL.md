Done: the person understands and can pass it on to someone who was not there.

People are visual and hold about seven new things at once; you hold far more, so use every means that helps. Every element must change what they understand, or it goes; say each thing once, where it is carried best: pictures carry the meaning themselves, not as an index to prose; words only what pictures cannot. Say what a thing is, never what it is like, labels included. Keep every claim, even shortened and read alone, within what the thing guarantees.

First win the reader least likely to stay: in seconds, from one picture simple enough for a glance, they see what this is, what it changes for whom, and why it is worth reading. Nothing they see first uses the system's own words: leave them out there, don't define them there. Then whole to parts, until a business reader and a specialist each reach their depth.

A key decision travels with its alternative, reason and accepted cost. People repeat only what stands out (title, first picture, headings), so each states its point and reason, a decision with its cost, never just names what follows.

Use the format the repo, team or field already accepts, else a widely read notation. Grow it from the conversation with the best tools at hand; views only this person sees are fine. The result stands alone in the form its destination needs: strict, every mark meaningful, nothing decorative or waiting for a click, its meaning intact there.

While you work and before it ships, someone without your context, seeing only what the reader gets, checks that every element is needed, every word literal, every claim true to scope and source, a stranger gets the subject and what it changes for whom from the first view alone, and each reader reaches their depth.
